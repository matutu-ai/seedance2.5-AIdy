# Camera Movement Library

## Dolly In

- **Definition:** Camera physically advances toward the subject.
- **Use:** discovery, pressure, intimacy, realization.
- **Emotion:** compresses space and intensifies attention.
- **Prompt:** “A 40 mm camera dollies in from medium shot to close-up over four seconds, decelerating into the reaction.”

## Dolly Out

- **Definition:** Camera withdraws from the subject.
- **Use:** isolation, aftermath, revealing context.
- **Emotion:** makes the subject feel smaller in the world.
- **Prompt:** “The camera slowly dollies backward from a close-up to a wide shot, revealing the empty platform behind him.”

## Tracking Shot

- **Definition:** Camera travels beside, ahead of, or behind a moving subject.
- **Use:** pursuit, journey, sustained action.
- **Emotion:** shares momentum without losing geography.
- **Prompt:** “At waist height, the camera tracks beside her at walking pace, holding a medium profile.”

## Orbit Shot

- **Definition:** Camera arcs around a fixed subject on a defined radius.
- **Use:** relationship reversal, spatial reveal.
- **Emotion:** reorients power and attention.
- **Prompt:** “The camera makes a controlled 90-degree left orbit around the couple, keeping their two-meter distance visible.”

## Crane Shot

- **Definition:** Camera rises or descends through a large vertical path.
- **Use:** scale, transition, establishing geography.
- **Emotion:** expands perspective.
- **Prompt:** “A crane rises from eye level to eight meters above the street, revealing the procession.”

## Handheld

- **Definition:** Operator-carried framing with restrained body-driven drift.
- **Use:** proximity, documentary urgency, instability.
- **Emotion:** embodied uncertainty.
- **Prompt:** “Subtle handheld breathing follows his turn; the frame settles after each step, without chaotic shake.”
