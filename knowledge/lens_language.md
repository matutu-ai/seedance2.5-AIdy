# Lens Language

| Tool | Spatial effect | Best use | Prompt cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close-up | isolates expression, compresses background | emotional turn | “85 mm close-up, eyes in sharp focus” |
| Medium shot | balances body and context | performance/blocking | “50 mm medium shot from waist height” |
| Wide shot | establishes scale and distance | geography, isolation | “28 mm wide shot holds the full corridor” |
| Macro | magnifies texture/detail | product or tactile clue | “100 mm macro on water beading across paint” |
| POV | places viewer at character attention | discovery, threat | “eye-level POV moves toward the half-open door” |
| Over-shoulder | binds people/object in a relationship | dialogue, looking | “50 mm over her shoulder toward the directory” |
| Tracking | preserves moving subject relationship | travel/pursuit | “side tracking at matching walking speed” |
| Handheld | introduces human operator presence | urgency, realism | “restrained handheld, small settling inertia” |

Use focal length only when it helps: wide lenses exaggerate near/far distance; longer lenses flatten space and isolate layers. Keep lens changes rare within a single shot.
