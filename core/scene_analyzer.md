# Scene Analyzer

Convert a simple plot into a shootable dramatic unit. Extract rather than decorate.

## Required analysis

```text
Scene genre: thriller / drama / fashion / commercial / other
Atmosphere: sensory conditions, pace, dominant tension
Character goal: what the protagonist tries to achieve now
Obstacle and conflict: what resists the goal
Emotional progression: start → escalation → peak → release/open end
Spatial premise: location, entrances, practical objects, character distances
Visual style: live-action reference qualities, color and texture
Camera style: motivated viewing strategy, not a generic adjective
```

## Method

1. Identify one visible action that changes the scene.
2. Locate the character's attention before selecting a shot.
3. Assign each beat a cause and an observable effect.
4. Limit a short generation to one primary dramatic turn. Split a longer story into separate shots when it needs multiple locations or irreversible time jumps.

## Example

**Premise:** A girl enters an abandoned hospital to find a missing person.

**Analysis:** Psychological thriller; rain-muted, decaying fluorescent atmosphere. Her immediate goal is to locate a ward directory without revealing herself. A distant metal impact makes the empty corridor feel occupied. She moves from alert restraint to contained fear, then commits to continuing. The camera starts behind her at the entrance and advances to her left profile as she finds the directory.
