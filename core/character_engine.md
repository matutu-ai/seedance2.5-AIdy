# Character Acting Engine

Do not render emotion as a label. Direct a performer through visible behavior that can evolve in time.

## Acting breakdown

For each principal character specify:

```text
Objective and inner pressure
Facial expression: brows, eyelids, jaw, lips
Eye behavior: target, scan, fixation, avoidance, refocus
Breathing: depth, rhythm, held breath, recovery
Posture and weight: spine, shoulders, lean, balance
Hands: tension, touch, fidget, release
Micro-expression: brief involuntary reveal
Transition: trigger and physical change between states
```

## Direction rules

- Give one playable action per beat; avoid stacking contradictory reactions.
- Use asymmetry and small delays: eyes often react before the head; breath can change before speech.
- Tie every movement to an object, partner, sound, or realization.
- Preserve identity details and wardrobe continuity when references are supplied.

## Translation example

Instead of “the girl is afraid,” write: “Her breathing shortens; her eyes sweep the dark doorways without moving her chin. Her brows draw together, lips part slightly, shoulders rise, and her fingers tighten around the flashlight. After the impact, her weight shifts half a step backward before she steadies herself.”
