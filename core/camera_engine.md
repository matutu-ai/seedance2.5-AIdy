# Camera Director Engine

Describe a physical camera operation with a narrative reason. “Cinematic” and “beautiful camera” are not instructions.

## Required camera specification

```text
Camera position: height, side, and relation to subject
Camera distance: framing at start and end
Lens: focal-length range or field-of-view character
Movement: path and direction
Speed: slow / measured / sudden, with a timed beat when useful
Focus change: subject A → subject B, or locked focus
Inertia: locked tripod, stabilized glide, subtle handheld breathing, or deliberate shake
```

## Direction rules

- Motivate movement by discovery, power shift, pursuit, or spatial revelation.
- Define the path relative to stable objects/people, not an abstract “camera circles.”
- Use one dominant move in a short shot. Add a focus pull only when it communicates a meaningful shift.
- Match instability to point of view: restrained handheld has body-weight drift, not random vibration.

**Example:** “At shoulder height, a 40 mm camera begins in a medium rear three-quarter shot two meters behind her. It dollies forward and left into a close left profile over six seconds, with subtle handheld breathing. Focus leaves the flashlight hand at second five and settles on her eyes.”
